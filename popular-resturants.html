Big menus

Popular Charleston Restaurants With Wide Menus

Not every meal is eggs. These are the dining rooms locals send visitors to when the group includes a seafood person, a burger person and someone who only wants a salad.

Southern Kitchens

Lowcountry Cooking With Something for Every Plate

Page's Okra Grill on Coleman Boulevard in Mount Pleasant may be the most recommended restaurant in the county, and the menu explains why. It covers breakfast, lunch and dinner, with shrimp and grits, fried green tomatoes, seafood platters, barbecue and burgers all coming out of one family-run kitchen. Expect a wait on the deck at peak hours. A second location serves Summerville.

Downtown, Poogan's Porch on Queen Street has been the classic first-night reservation for decades, with fried chicken, she-crab soup and a weekend brunch in a restored house. Magnolias on East Bay Street takes the same pantry in a more polished direction, and its neighbor Slightly North of Broad builds a long menu around what local farms and boats bring in. 82 Queen rounds out the group with courtyard seating and a deep list of Lowcountry standards.

Seafood Houses

Fried, Steamed, Raw and Everything Between

Hyman's Seafood on Meeting Street is the definition of a big menu: pages of fish you can order several ways, plus po'boys, pasta and sides, in a building the same family has worked for generations. On the harbor, Fleet Landing occupies a former Navy building over the water at 186 Concord Street and pairs the view with a menu that runs from oysters to fried chicken.

Charleston Crab House leans into steam pots and raw bar platters and seats large groups without fuss. For a looser night out, Leon's Oyster Shop on Upper King splits its menu between fried chicken and chargrilled oysters, which settles most disagreements before they start.

Barbecue & Pubs

Smokehouses and Taprooms With Deep Menus

Charleston's barbecue row sits on the upper peninsula. Lewis Barbecue on North Nassau Street serves Texas-style brisket, hot guts and a full lineup of sides by the pound, while Rodney Scott's BBQ keeps whole-hog South Carolina barbecue at the center and fills in around it with ribs, wings and catfish. Home Team BBQ and Swig & Swine both run several locations around town with menus that stretch from smoked wings to tacos and salads.

Edmund's Oast on Morrison Drive is the pick when half the table wants craft beer and the other half wants a real dinner; the kitchen is as serious as the tap list.

West Ashley & The Islands

Neighborhood Favorites Off the Peninsula

California Dreaming sits on the Ashley River in West Ashley and has one of the longest menus around, with steaks, ribs, seafood and oversized salads in a room with water on three sides. In Avondale, Triangle Char & Bar covers burgers, bowls and tacos and does a busy weekend brunch, and Kickin' Chicken is the local wings-and-wraps standby with locations across the area.

On James Island, Bohemian Bull on Folly Road is a beer garden with a large burger-led menu and room for a crowd. Out on Sullivan's Island, The Obstinate Daughter mixes pizza, pasta and local seafood and is among the most consistent kitchens in the region.

Start the Day Downtown First

Most of these rooms don't open until lunch. For the hours before, or for a late breakfast in the middle of a King Street afternoon, Sweet Palm Coffee & Food at 471 King Street keeps its morning menu going until close. The rest of the any-hour list is on the area guide.
